I would transcribe it, with only minor normalization, as:

> **NB Diese 3 vorstehende sind d. 5t Decembris 1757 in der Schlacht bey
> Leuthen ohnweit Breslau auf den Platz todt geblieben und daselbst
> eingescharret, hieselbst aber auf vielfältiges Begehren denselben auf 2
> Sonntagen die Leichen-Predigten gehalten, der letzte auch beläutet
> worden.**

Translation:

> **Note: These three persons listed above were killed on the spot on 5
> December 1757 in the Battle of Leuthen, not far from Breslau, and were
> buried there. Here, however, at repeated request, funeral sermons were
> preached for them on two Sundays; for the last one the church bells were
> also rung.**

A few points: **auf den Platz todt geblieben** means essentially “were
killed on the spot” or “died there on the battlefield.” **eingescharret**
is literally “scraped/dug into the ground,” hence “buried there,” probably
implying battlefield burial rather than a normal churchyard burial.
**beläutet** means that the bells were rung for him.

So these were almost certainly **three Petzen-area soldiers who died at the
Battle of Leuthen**, whose bodies remained buried in Silesia; the Petzen
parish later provided local memorial observances for them.

The three men had been **killed at the Battle of Leuthen on December 5,
1757**, and were **buried there near the battlefield**, not brought back to
Petzen. Nevertheless, in Petzen, **funeral sermons were later preached for
them at the repeated request of their families or others**, apparently over
two Sundays. For the last of the three, **the church bells were also
rung**.

So these were essentially **memorial funeral observances in Petzen for men
whose bodies were buried elsewhere**.
