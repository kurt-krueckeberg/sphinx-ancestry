# Johann Heinrich Krückeberg (1744-1822) Event Table

## Introduction

On 21 March 1822, a Johann Heinrich Krückeberg died who was a Leibzüchter, a retired farmer who:

- has handed the farm over to his heir,
- but still has a lifetime right to housing, support, and use of part of the property.

His age is given as 78 years, 2 months and 3 weeks. Subtracting this precise
age from 21 March 1822 places his birth on 31 December 1743, a date on which
no births occurred in the Petzen parish. However, on 19 January 1744, a
Johann Heinrich Krückeberg was born, which is 78 years, 2 months and 3 days
(not 3 weeks) from 21 March 1822. Did the scribe accidently write "weeks"
instead of "days"?  Can we in any other way further establish that the Johann
Heinrich Krückeberg who was born 19 January 1744 is the same Johann Heinrich
Krückeberg who died on 21 March 1822?

Further points about Johann Heinrich are, on {external+genealogy:doc}`7 November 1773 the widower Johann Heinrich Krückeberg from Berenbusch marries marries Ilse Anna Marie Elisabeth née Schakel <petzen/PET-M-1773a>`. The odds of there
being two Johann Heinrich Krückebergs from Berenbusch who are both widowers
in November, 1773, negligible.

## Facts

- He dies at age of 78 years, 2 months and 3 weeks (maybe 3 days).
- He is mentioned as a Leibzüchter when he {external+genealogy:doc}`marries on 11 April 1802 <petzen/PET-M-1802b>` and when he
{external+genealogy:doc}`dies on 21 March 1822 <petzen/PET-D-1822a>`. For both events, his address is no. 10 Berenbusch.
However, when his wife is {external+genealogy:doc}`buried on 6 January 1819 <petzen/PET-D-1819a>` "he" is described as

> vormaligen Hofmeisters auf dem Reinen-Neelhof Johann Heinrich Krückeberg, Einlieger beÿ n.13 in Bärenbusch

The addresses are different. We know that no. 13 Berenbusch was where the
husband of Johann Heinrich’s daughter Maria Philippine Krückeberg.
Friedrich Wilhelm Raulfing, lived, as their
{external+genealogy:doc}`wedding ceremony on 10 May 1812 <petzen/PET-M-1812a>` shows. It is
the "Place" where his wife, Philippine Dorothea nee Beucke died. This was
the "most recent residence of the deceased". So if Johann Heinrich was
staying there, too, at no. 13 Berenbusch.  Maybe that is why he is called an
"Einlieger bei (at) no. 13 Berenbusch". He was a "tenant" there temporarily?

- The Johann Heinrich Krückeberg who married Ilse Anna Marie Elisabeth Schäkeln Schakele 7 Nov. 1773
was a
  1. widower and
  2. from Berenbusch.
- Is the first born son named after the paternal grandfather? Is this naming pattern (of the first
paternal grandson being named after his patern grandfather) evident?
- The Johann Heinrich Krückeberg who married Philippine Dorothea Beucken on 11 April 1802
was a
  1. widower
  2. Leibzüchter
  3. lived a no. 10 Berenbusch.
- But when Philippine Dorothee née Beucke Krückeberg dies, her burial record states her husband, Johann
Heinrich Krückeberg is an **Einlieger beÿ n.13 in Bärenbusch**. Most of the records involving Johann
Heinrich refer to **no. 10 Berenbusch** not **no.13 Berenbusch**.
  **NOTE**: The Bückeburg archive has: Stätte no. 13 Berenbusch.
- At his death in 1822, he is only survived by two sons. Yet the Johann Heinrich Krückeberg, who married
Ilse Anna Marie Elisabeth Schekel, had apparently three children.

### The Leibzüchter Fact

Let’s begin with the "Leibzüchter" fact.  We will use the table below that when filled out should reveal:

1. His son **Jobst Heinrich remained all his adult life at No. 10 Berenbusch**
2. His son **Johann Heinrich relocated permanently to No. 19 Evesen through marriage**
3. **The father died at the ancestral Hof (No. 10)**
4. **Jobst Heinrich died at the same Hof**, confirming succession
5. Their children were raised on **two separate household lines**

This is provide proof that **Jobst Heinrich was the inheriting son**
* **Johann Heinrich founded a cadet branch in Evesen**
